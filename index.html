About the book

The workplace does not have to be a place people recover from.

Burnout, toxic cultures, chronic urgency, and performative wellbeing are often treated as separate workplace problems. Heal the Workplace examines the systems, leadership habits, and everyday choices that allow these conditions to take root — and the practical work required to change them.

Drawing on research, lived experience, and conversations with leaders, founders, employees, psychologists, researchers, and caregivers, the book explores how healthier organizations are built intentionally. Alongside difficult workplace realities, it shares stories of people and organizations choosing more sustainable ways to work.

The book moves from recognizing burnout and toxicity to recovery, cultural repair, new models of work, and the inner work of leadership. Its focus is not only on what is broken, but on how workplaces can become healthier, more humane, and built to endure.

  • 01How can leaders recognize burnout before it becomes overwhelming?
  • 02What causes workplace cultures to become toxic, and what does meaningful repair require?
  • 03How can organizations replace chronic urgency with sustainable performance?
  • 04What can new work models and AI teach us about redesigning work?
  • 05How can wellbeing become part of everyday leadership rather than a standalone initiative?

About the author

Subramanian (Subbu) S. Kalpathi

Subramanian S. Kalpathi is the author of Heal the Workplace: Charting a Path from Burnout and Toxicity to Organizational Longevity and the host of the Culture Matters podcast. His work explores workplace culture, leadership, mental health, and the choices through which organizations become healthier and more sustainable.

Culture Matters Podcast

Conversations on the forces that shape how people experience work

Culture Matters is a podcast about the forces that shape how people experience work — from leadership and organizational behaviour to mental health, culture, and the future of work. Through conversations with researchers, leaders, practitioners, and people with lived experience, the podcast explores how workplaces can become more thoughtful and humane.
